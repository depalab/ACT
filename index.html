Teaching the next generation to code & build with AI.

ACT — AI Assisted Coding and Tooling — is an educational program of the Center for Equitable AI and Machine Learning Systems (CEAMLS) at Morgan State University. We bring AI-assisted coding skills to students, schools, and universities through monthly workshops, an annual national symposium, and community events.

01 Mission

AI is rewriting how we work and learn.
No student should be left behind.

ACT exists to make AI-assisted coding and tooling skills accessible to every learner — from middle school classrooms to university research labs. As part of CEAMLS at Morgan State University, we are committed to equitable AI education that prepares students, educators, and communities to use these tools thoughtfully, effectively, and ethically.

/01

Access

We bring high-quality AI education directly to student organizations, K–12 schools, and universities — on their campus, on their schedule, at no cost where possible.

  • Workshops in schools and student orgs
  • Materials provided
  • Open to all skill levels
/02

Skills

Hands-on training in real AI-assisted coding tools — the same tools used by professional engineers.

/03

Community

Learn & Lunch sessions, peer cohorts, and the NSEA symposium connect learners across institutions.

/04

Equity

Backed by Morgan State's Center for Equitable AI and Machine Learning Systems, our work centers students and institutions that have historically been under-resourced in AI education — especially HBCUs, public schools, and first-generation learners.

  • HBCU-led and HBCU-serving
  • Accessible, plain-language curriculum
  • No prior coding required
02 Programs

Three ways we teach.

ACT runs three program tracks, each meeting learners where they are — from a single lunchtime session on campus to a two-day national symposium.

P/01

Monthly Workshops

Year-round

Hands-on, one-to-two-hour workshops delivered through student organizations, K–12 schools, and universities — including the Learn & Lunch series hosted by CEAMLS at Morgan State. Topics rotate across AI-assisted coding, prompt engineering, building with LLMs, and responsible AI use.

FormatIn-person or virtual
AudienceStudents & educators
CostFree for partners
P/02

NSEA — National Symposium on Effective & Ethical AI

Annual flagship

Our annual national convening, hosted by CEAMLS at Morgan State University. NSEA brings together researchers, educators, policymakers, students, and industry leaders to examine how AI is designed, deployed, and governed. The 2026 theme is Human-AI Collaboration: Empowerment, Agency, and Belonging.

Next eventJuly 25–26, 2026
LocationMorgan State, Baltimore
Websiteequitableaisymposium.com

03 NSEA 2026

National Symposium on Effective & Ethical AI.

The flagship annual event of CEAMLS and a centerpiece of the ACT program. Two days of keynotes, research presentations, and conversations on how to build AI systems that empower people rather than replace them.

  1. Theme

    Human-AI Collaboration

    Empowerment, Agency, and Belonging — how AI systems can augment human capability while preserving human decision-making authority, dignity, and meaningful participation.

  2. When

    July 25–26, 2026

    Two days of programming — keynotes, panels, paper sessions, and networking with researchers, educators, students, and industry leaders.

  3. Where

    Morgan State University, Baltimore, MD

    Hosted on the Morgan State campus by the Center for Equitable AI and Machine Learning Systems (CEAMLS).

  4. Who

    Distinguished speakers from across the field

    20+ featured speakers from leading universities, industry, and government agencies — alongside emerging student researchers presenting their own work.

  5. Register

    Open to all

    Students, faculty, educators, industry professionals, and policymakers are all welcome. Visit equitableaisymposium.com for registration, the full program, and the speaker lineup.

05 Why this matters

AI is no longer a specialist tool — it is becoming part of how everyone reads, writes, codes, and learns. The students who learn to use it well, ethically, and creatively will lead the next generation of work. ACT makes sure those students reflect every community we serve.
